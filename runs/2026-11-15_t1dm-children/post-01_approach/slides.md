# New diabetes in a child: the work-up in order

## Slide 1: One reading can be enough
**Headline:** Symptoms plus random 11.1 mmol/L: diagnosed today
**Body:** Classic symptoms plus a random venous glucose of 11.1 mmol/L or more diagnose diabetes. If symptoms are not unequivocal, confirm by repeat testing. An OGTT is seldom needed.
**Visual:** One bold glucose number on a clean card beside a symptom checklist icon.
**Source:** p.8

## Slide 2: Ask about night wetting, not just thirst
**Headline:** Ask about night wetting, not just thirst
**Body:** Over 90% have polyuria and polydipsia, yet these often surface only when you ask about wet nappies, night wetting, new daytime wetting or thirst. The child may be hypovolaemic without looking dry.
**Visual:** A question mark over a bed icon, with a small water-drop icon.
**Source:** p.5

## Slide 3: Under-3s: over half arrive in DKA
**Headline:** Under-3s: over half arrive in DKA
**Body:** DKA is the first presentation in about 30% of children, range 15 to 70%. Risk is higher under 6 years and with socioeconomic disadvantage. Over half of under-3s present this way.
**Visual:** An age ladder with the under-3 rung highlighted.
**Source:** p.6

## Slide 4: Draw antibodies before the first insulin dose
**Headline:** Draw antibodies before the first insulin dose
**Body:** Send ZnT8, GAD65, IAA and IA-2 at presentation, ideally before the first insulin dose or soon after. Insulin antibodies may appear about 10 to 14 days after insulin is first given.
**Visual:** A syringe beside four labelled sample tubes, with a clock icon showing timing.
**Source:** p.9

## Slide 5: HbA1c looks back 10 to 12 weeks
**Headline:** HbA1c looks back 10 to 12 weeks
**Body:** Confirm HbA1c of 6.5% or more with a second measure. Fasting glucose of 7.0 mmol/L or more on two occasions also counts. Haemoglobin variants and rapid red-cell turnover mislead HbA1c.
**Visual:** A 10 to 12 week calendar strip with a glucose drop-line, no numbers on the icon.
**Source:** p.8

## Slide 6: Antibody-negative T1DM is still type 1B
**Headline:** Antibody-negative T1DM is still type 1B
**Body:** Most T1DM shows islet antibodies, the type 1A pattern. Clinical T1DM with no detectable antibodies and no other cause is type 1B.
**Visual:** A two-branch tree: antibody positive to 1A, antibody negative to 1B.
**Source:** p.10

## Slide 7: Measure C-peptide once the child has recovered
**Headline:** Measure C-peptide once the child has recovered
**Body:** If T2DM is possible, measure insulin and C-peptide after recovery from the hyperglycaemic stress. Glucotoxicity and acute illness can suppress both. Low for the glucose points to T1DM; high points to T2DM.
**Visual:** A timeline with an acute-illness block and a recovery arrow leading to a lab tube.
**Source:** p.10

## Slide 8: Steroids and critical illness can raise glucose
**Headline:** Steroids and critical illness can raise glucose
**Body:** Diabetes is the main cause of significant hyperglycaemia in a previously well child. Non-diabetic causes: critical illness, high-dose steroids, acute sympathomimetics. Drug-induced diabetes can follow atypical antipsychotics, tacrolimus or glucocorticoids.
**Visual:** A stack of three drug-pill icons beside a single-lens magnifier over a glucose line.
**Source:** p.10, p.11, p.12

## Slide 9: Glycosuria with normal glucose is not diabetes
**Headline:** Glycosuria with normal glucose is not diabetes
**Body:** Renal glycosuria and Fanconi syndrome both spill glucose into urine while plasma glucose stays normal. Check plasma glucose before reading urine glucose as diabetes.
**Visual:** A urine dipstick with a question mark on the glucose pad.
**Source:** p.12

## Slide 10: Glucose, antibodies, HbA1c, C-peptide, then cause
**Headline:** Glucose, antibodies, HbA1c, C-peptide, then cause
**Body:** Working order, not a protocol: symptoms plus glucose 11.1 mmol/L or more; antibodies before insulin; HbA1c limits; type 1A or 1B; C-peptide after recovery; then non-diabetic and drug causes. Save for your next new-onset child.
**Visual:** A numbered staircase of six steps with a save bookmark at the top.
**Source:** p.8, p.9, p.10, p.12
