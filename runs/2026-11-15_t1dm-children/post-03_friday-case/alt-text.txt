Slide 2: What is the next step? What is the most appropriate next step? Visual: A single question mark over a clinical checklist, with one box ticked.

Slide 3: Five plausible next steps. A. Oral rehydration trial and discharge as gastroenteritis. B. Topical antifungal and review in a week. C. Capillary blood glucose and urine glucose now. D. Stool PCR and review. E. Overnight dexamethasone suppression test. Visual: Five full-width rows stacked top to bottom, labelled A to E.

Slide 4: The clue is the wet nappies. Answer C. Urine output is preserved, with heavier, more frequent wet nappies, during 4 days of vomiting. Dehydration would normally reduce urine output. Candidiasis supports the answer but does not decide it alone. Visual: A wet-nappy icon with a downward arrow crossed out, showing urine output is not falling.

Slide 5: Why the others fail, and the pearl. A: glycosuric polyuria masks the usual falling urine output. B: candidiasis should prompt a glucose check. D: no diarrhoeal clue. E: Cushing hyperglycaemia is very rare in children; no growth change is described. Visual: Five small cards, with a cross on each wrong option (A, B, D and E) and a tick on option C, the correct answer.
