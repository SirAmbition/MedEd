# Fictional case: wet nappies and a vomiting toddler

## Slide 1: The toddler who was labelled gastro
**Headline:** The toddler labelled gastro
**Body:** Fictional case. 2-year-old girl, 4 days of vomiting, labelled gastroenteritis at a GP visit. Mum reports heavier, more frequent wet nappies. Recurrent nappy candidiasis for 2 months. HR 132/min, capillary refill 2 s.

## Slide 3: Five plausible next steps
**Headline:** Five plausible next steps
**Body:** A. Oral rehydration trial and discharge as gastroenteritis. B. Topical antifungal and review in a week. C. Capillary blood glucose and urine glucose now. D. Stool PCR and review. E. Overnight dexamethasone suppression test.
**Visual:** Five labelled cards, A to E, in a row, with no text inside icons.
**Source:** p.6, p.7, p.11, p.12

## Slide 4: The clue is the wet nappies
**Headline:** The clue is the wet nappies
**Body:** Answer C. Urine output is preserved, with heavier, more frequent wet nappies, during 4 days of vomiting. Dehydration would normally reduce urine output. Candidiasis supports the answer but does not decide it alone.
**Visual:** A wet-nappy icon with a downward arrow crossed out, to show urine output is not falling.
**Source:** p.6, p.7

## Slide 5: Why the others fail, and the pearl
**Headline:** Why the others fail, and the pearl
**Body:** A: glycosuric polyuria masks the usual falling urine output. B: candidiasis should prompt a glucose check. D: no diarrhoeal clue. E: Cushing hyperglycaemia is very rare in children; no growth change is described.
**Visual:** Five small cards with a red cross on each wrong option, one green tick on C.
**Source:** p.6, p.7, p.11, p.12, p.14

## Key claims

| Claim | Source page |
|---|---|
| Vomiting illness can mimic DKA because urine output is masked by glycosuric polyuria | p.6 |
| Prolonged or recurrent nappy candidiasis should prompt glucose checking | p.7 |
| Young child with dehydration, abdominal pain or fatigue: check glucose and urine glucose | p.7 |
| Glucose and urine glucose measurement recommended in young children with these symptoms | p.14 |
| Cushing syndrome concern when growth velocity falls despite increasing weight; hyperglycaemia very rare in childhood Cushing | p.12 |
