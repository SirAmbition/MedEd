Slide 1: No single test separates youth diabetes types. Neither one criterion nor one test reliably separates T1DM from T2DM. Each slide marks whether a feature separates the types or overlaps them. Visual: Two overlapping circles, one for T1DM and one for T2DM, with a shared centre where they overlap.

Slide 2: Weight overlaps; age under 10 does not. Up to 25% of T1DM are overweight. T2DM is usually a BMI at or above the 95th centile. About 45% of T1DM presents before age 10, while nearly all T2DM presents after age 10. Visual: Two bars: a wide overlap band for weight, and for age a T1DM band spanning both sides of age 10 with a T2DM band sitting only after it.

Slide 3: Up to 30% of clinical T2DM has antibodies. Some clinical T2DM carries islet autoantibodies and may be a slowly progressive autoimmune diabetes. Antibodies overlap between the types, so they do not separate them on their own. Visual: A T2DM tile with a small antibody marker covering part of it, outlined to show the overlap.

Slide 4: Acanthosis separates, but not cleanly. Blood tests show insulin resistance in up to 25% of clinically diagnosed T1DM. Acanthosis nigricans in 50 to 90% of US youth with T2DM, against about 12% with T1DM. Visual: Two stacked bars: a large T2DM bar and a small T1DM bar, joined by a dotted overlap line.

Slide 5: One in five US registry youth had both. In one US registry: both autoimmunity and insulin resistance 20%, typical of obesity with T1DM; autoimmune and insulin sensitive 55%; non-autoimmune and insulin resistant 16%; neither feature 10%. Visual: A four-quadrant grid with autoimmunity on one axis and insulin resistance on the other, showing the four groups.

Slide 6: Family history: a wide gap between the types. A close relative is affected in up to 10% of T1DM, against 75 to 90% of T2DM. Take the family history before classifying the diabetes. Visual: A family tree with one close relative highlighted, with the two figures from the body text beside it.

Slide 7: Consider monogenic diabetes in this pattern. Think monogenic if under 25, not insulin-dependent, no islet autoimmunity or acanthosis, dominant over three generations. Confirm by sequencing. Also consider it in insulin-sensitive youth without islet autoimmunity (10% of one registry). Visual: A DNA strand beside a three-generation family tree, with a gene-test tube icon.
