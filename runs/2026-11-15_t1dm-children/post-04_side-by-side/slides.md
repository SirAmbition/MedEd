# Youth T1DM, T2DM and monogenic: where they overlap

## Slide 1: No single test separates the types
**Headline:** No single test separates youth diabetes types
**Body:** Neither one criterion nor one test reliably separates T1DM from T2DM. Each slide marks whether a feature separates the types or overlaps them.
**Visual:** Two overlapping circles labelled T1DM and T2DM, with a shared centre.
**Source:** p.9

## Slide 2: Weight overlaps; age under 10 does not
**Headline:** Weight overlaps; age under 10 does not
**Body:** Up to 25% of T1DM are overweight. T2DM is usually a BMI at or above the 95th centile. About 45% of T1DM presents before age 10, while nearly all T2DM presents after age 10.
**Visual:** Two bars: a wide overlap band for weight; for age, T1DM spans both sides of 10 and T2DM sits only after it.
**Source:** p.9

## Slide 3: Up to 30% of clinical T2DM has antibodies
**Headline:** Up to 30% of clinical T2DM has antibodies
**Body:** Some clinical T2DM carries islet autoantibodies and may be a slowly progressive autoimmune diabetes. Antibodies overlap between the types, so they do not separate them on their own.
**Visual:** A T2DM tile with a small antibody marker on a portion of it, labelled as an overlap.
**Source:** p.10

## Slide 4: Acanthosis separates, but not cleanly
**Headline:** Acanthosis separates, but not cleanly
**Body:** Blood tests show insulin resistance in up to 25% of clinically diagnosed T1DM. Acanthosis nigricans in 50 to 90% of US youth with T2DM, against about 12% with T1DM.
**Visual:** Two stacked bars, a large T2DM bar and a small T1DM bar, with a dotted overlap line.
**Source:** p.9

## Slide 5: One in five US registry youth had both
**Headline:** One in five US registry youth had both
**Body:** In one US registry: both autoimmunity and insulin resistance 20%, typical of obesity with T1DM; autoimmune and insulin sensitive 55%; non-autoimmune and insulin resistant 16%; neither feature 10%.
**Visual:** A four-quadrant grid with autoimmune on one axis and insulin resistance on the other.
**Source:** p.10

## Slide 6: Family history: a wide gap between the types
**Headline:** Family history: a wide gap between the types
**Body:** A close relative is affected in up to 10% of T1DM, against 75 to 90% of T2DM. Take the family history before classifying the diabetes.
**Visual:** A family tree with one highlighted relative, labelled with both figures.
**Source:** p.9

## Slide 7: Consider monogenic diabetes in this pattern
**Headline:** Consider monogenic diabetes in this pattern
**Body:** Think monogenic if under 25, not insulin-dependent, no islet autoimmunity or acanthosis, dominant over three generations. Confirm by sequencing. Also consider it in insulin-sensitive youth without islet autoimmunity (10% of one registry).
**Visual:** A DNA strand beside a three-generation family tree, with a gene-test tube icon.
**Source:** p.10, p.11
