# Youth T1DM, T2DM and monogenic: where they overlap

## Slide 1: No single test separates the types
**Headline:** No single test separates youth diabetes types
**Body:** No set of criteria or diagnostic test consistently distinguishes T1DM from T2DM. Each slide below marks whether a feature separates the types or overlaps them.
**Visual:** Two overlapping circles labelled T1DM and T2DM, with a shared centre.
**Source:** p.9

## Slide 2: Weight overlaps; age under 10 does not
**Headline:** Weight overlaps; age under 10 does not
**Body:** Up to 25% of T1DM are overweight, and T2DM is usually BMI 95th centile or more. About 45% of T1DM presents before age 10; almost all T2DM presents after 10.
**Visual:** Two bars: a wide overlap band for weight; for age, T1DM spans both sides of 10 and T2DM sits only after it.
**Source:** p.9

## Slide 3: Up to 30% of T2DM is antibody positive
**Headline:** Up to 30% of T2DM is antibody positive
**Body:** The clinical T2DM phenotype can carry islet autoantibodies, a slowly progressive autoimmune diabetes. Antibodies overlap between the types, so they do not separate them on their own.
**Visual:** A T2DM tile with a small antibody marker on a portion of it, labelled as an overlap.
**Source:** p.10

## Slide 4: Acanthosis separates, but not cleanly
**Headline:** Acanthosis separates, but not cleanly
**Body:** Acanthosis nigricans in 50 to 90% of US youth with T2DM, against about 12% with T1DM. Up to 25% of clinically diagnosed T1DM have biochemical insulin resistance.
**Visual:** Two stacked bars, a large T2DM bar and a small T1DM bar, with a dotted overlap line.
**Source:** p.9

## Slide 5: One in five US registry youth had both
**Headline:** One in five US registry youth had both
**Body:** In one US registry, 55% were autoimmune and insulin sensitive; 16% were non-autoimmune and insulin resistant. 20% had both, a pattern typical of obesity with T1DM. 10% had neither. These are cohort shares only.
**Visual:** A four-quadrant grid with autoimmune on one axis and insulin resistance on the other.
**Source:** p.10

## Slide 6: Family history separates better than weight
**Headline:** Family history separates better than weight
**Body:** Up to 10% of T1DM have an affected close relative, against 75 to 90% of T2DM. Take the family history before you type the diabetes.
**Visual:** A family tree with one highlighted relative, labelled with both figures.
**Source:** p.9

## Slide 7: Consider monogenic diabetes in this pattern
**Headline:** Consider monogenic diabetes in this pattern
**Body:** Monogenic: under 25, non-insulin-dependent, autosomal dominant across three generations, no islet autoantibodies, no acanthosis. Confirm by gene sequencing. Consider it in the 10% with no autoimmunity or insulin resistance, mainly those labelled T2DM.
**Visual:** A DNA strand beside a three-generation family tree, with a gene-test tube icon.
**Source:** p.10, p.11
